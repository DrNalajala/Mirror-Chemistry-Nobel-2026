The puzzle

Left hand, right hand, one biology

A carbon atom carrying four different groups can exist as two non-superimposable mirror images, the enantiomers. They share melting point, NMR and solubility, yet a chiral receptor, enzyme or ion channel tells them apart.

Life uses L-amino acids and D-sugars almost exclusively. For over a century chemists could not explain how such one-handedness (homochirality) arises, because ordinary reactions starting from achiral or racemic material give exactly 50:50 mixtures. The 2026 laureates found the two missing mechanisms: non-linear effects, where a small chiral bias is magnified by catalyst aggregation, and asymmetric autocatalysis, where a product copies its own handedness.

Wedge = toward you, dashes = away. Reflecting through the mirror swaps R and S; no rotation turns one into the other.

Henri B. Kagan · 1986

When 50% ee in the catalyst gives 80% ee in the product

Textbook logic says a catalyst at 50% ee should give a product at half its maximum ee. Kagan and co-workers tested this in 1986 and found it often fails. Plotting product ee against ligand ee gave curves, not straight lines.

The cause is aggregation. If the active catalyst contains two chiral ligands (Kagan's ML2 model), three species form: two homochiral complexes and one heterochiral "meso" complex. When the meso complex is unusually stable and sluggish, it locks away the minor ligand. The remaining active catalyst is far purer than the ligand pool, so the product ee overshoots: a positive non-linear effect, or asymmetric amplification. When the meso complex is the more reactive one, product ee falls below the line: a negative non-linear effect.

Large K and small g: the meso complex acts as a reservoir for the minority ligand.

Kenso Soai · Nature, 1995

A molecule that makes more of itself, and more of its own hand

Soai added diisopropylzinc to 2-substituted pyrimidine-5-carbaldehydes. The product, a pyrimidyl alkanol (as its zinc alkoxide), turned out to be a highly enantioselective catalyst for its own formation. Seeded with product of tiny ee, it generated new product of far higher ee, which in turn catalysed the next batch. Starting from about 0.00005% ee, three consecutive rounds reached above 99.5% ee.

The 2-alkynylpyrimidine version gives the strongest amplification. The blue loop: product (as Zn alkoxide) returns as catalyst. Which product hand forms depends on which hand seeds the reaction; the S label here is illustrative.

Absolute asymmetric synthesis

Start with nothing chiral, end with one hand

Run the Soai reaction with no chiral seed at all and each flask still ends highly enriched, sometimes R, sometimes S. The tiny statistical imbalance from the first racemic background molecules is amplified. Over many flasks the outcomes split roughly evenly: spontaneous mirror-symmetry breaking.

What can tip the balance

Physical chirality

Circularly polarised light, and chiral crystals of quartz or sodium chlorate, have been reported to decide which hand wins.

Isotope chirality

Compounds chiral only because of H/D or 12C/13C substitution have been reported to trigger the reaction: an extraordinarily sensitive chirality detector.

Mechanism, still argued

Recent work proposes a homochiral tetrameric Zn alkoxide (Denmark, Nat. Chem. 2020) and transient hemiacetal zinc complexes (Trapp, Nat. Commun. 2025) as key catalytic species.

From origin-of-life puzzle to plant floor

What the prize means for API process chemistry

Most new small-molecule drugs are single enantiomers, and the minor enantiomer is controlled as an impurity. Non-linear effects and autocatalysis change how chemists set ligand specifications, diagnose catalysts, and think about ee upgrade.

cost & supply

Ligand specifications can be set rationally

With a positive NLE, a ligand at 90–95% ee can deliver product ee close to the 100% ligand result, so a cheaper or less rigorously resolved ligand lot may be acceptable. With a negative NLE, ligand optical purity becomes a critical material attribute. The Ligand-ee planner tab turns this into numbers.

mechanism

A five-point NLE plot is a cheap mechanistic probe

Running the reaction at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% ligand ee reveals whether the active catalyst is a monomer or an aggregate. That informs scale-up risks: concentration sensitivity, induction periods, sensitivity to water or additives that break aggregates, and catalyst loading choices.

quality

Trace chirality can steer outcomes

Soai's reaction shows that a vanishingly small chiral bias can dominate the final result when a process amplifies. For amplifying or autocatalytic steps, control strategies should consider chiral contaminants, seeds from previous batches in shared equipment, and enantiomeric purity of recycled streams.

ee upgrade

Amplification as a design principle

Asymmetric amplification sits alongside crystallisation-based ee upgrades (preferential crystallisation, attrition-enhanced deracemisation) as ways to turn modest selectivity into >99% ee. Autocatalytic amplification also inspires ultra-sensitive chiral sensing for enantiomeric impurity work.
